Designer and design help

How do I start designing my kitchen?

Begin with how you use the room — where you prep, cook, clean up, and store things — and with the measurements you have. You can start from a kitchen look or a ready-to-customize Kitchen Package, then open the Designer and place those work areas on your walls. Style and finish are easier to settle once the layout works. Room measurements help you organize the plan; a local CabinetDealz store can confirm exact numbers before anything is ordered.

What do I need to measure before I start?

Measure each wall corner to corner, the ceiling height, and every window, door, and opening from the nearest corner. Note where the sink, range, refrigerator, outlets, and water lines sit today. Rough numbers are enough for a first layout; exact measurements should be confirmed before an order is placed.

What is the kitchen work triangle, and does it still matter?

It is the path between the sink, range, and refrigerator. A common planning guideline is to keep each leg between 4 and 9 feet, and the three sides together between 13 and 26 feet, so you are not crossing the room for every step. Avoid letting a walkway or island cut through that path.

How much space do I need between counters or around an island?

Plan on at least 42 inches of walkway for one cook and 48 inches if two people work at once. Measure from the front edge of the countertop, not only the cabinet box — a standard 1.5 inch overhang on each side uses 3 inches of the aisle. Also check that dishwasher, oven, and refrigerator doors can open without blocking the walkway.

How should I handle corners, and why is a filler on my quote?

Two standard cabinets cannot meet squarely in a corner without their doors or drawers hitting. Common options are a lazy Susan, a diagonal corner, or a blind corner with a pull-out. A filler is a narrow matching strip that closes a gap, usually at a wall or beside a corner so a neighboring drawer can open. It is a real part, cut to fit. The Designer applies the cabinet geometry and fillers for the corner you choose. If a plan shows several fillers in the middle of a run, the cabinet widths can often be chosen more cleanly.

Why is the finished countertop deeper than a 24-inch cabinet?

Standard base cabinets are 24 inches deep. The countertop typically adds a 1.5 inch overhang past the doors, for a finished depth around 25.5 inches. That is the number your tape measure will hit, and the number to use when you check aisle clearance.
